Meet Mía

A five-year-old fighting with joy

Mía loves: dancing, painting, ballet, acrobatics, music — and caring for the people she loves.

To Mía

Mía has been a remarkable little girl since the day she was born.

Read more Read less ▾

Even as a toddler, she surprised everyone with her maturity, her sensitivity, and the way she instinctively looked out for others. When she was barely two, her great-grandmother slipped from a chair, and tiny as she was, Mía ran over and tried with all her strength to help her up. That's Mía — a huge heart inside a tiny little body.

She's fascinated by music and dance. In fact, she started dancing before she learned to walk — music would come on while she was still in her playpen, and she'd start moving right away.

She loves painting, ballet, and acrobatics. She has an incredible gift for music: she hears a song and quickly learns the words, but what's truly remarkable is that she doesn't just memorize it — she understands what the song means and what it's trying to say.

Mía also carries a sensitivity and maturity that often make us forget how young she really is.

She's deeply loving toward her mom — always checking in on her, asking how she's feeling, worrying about her health. She's watched her mom go through hard times with chronic fatigue and other physical struggles, and so often Mía becomes her mom's own little caretaker.

And she prays for her. With all the beautiful innocence of a little girl, she asks God to make her mommy feel better.

That's Mía. She loves her whole family.

A joyful, artistic, intelligent, sensitive little girl, full of love. A little girl who loves dancing, singing, painting, and caring for the people she loves. A little one with an incredible capacity to understand and feel for others.

Before cancer entered our lives, Mía was already a little fighter. And she's the reason we're standing here today, ready to fight with everything we have.

To Sonia

Help me support the one who never stops fighting for Mía. When we talk about Mía, we talk about a little girl full of light, dreams, joy, and an immense will to live. A little girl who today is facing one of the hardest battles a human being can face: the fight against cancer.

Read more Read less ▾

But behind Mía there's someone whose story I also want to tell.

Her mom — my daughter — Sonia Rivas.

From the day we received Mía's diagnosis, Sonia's life changed completely. Since then, almost every minute of her days has one purpose: to do everything humanly possible to save her daughter.

Sonia is the one by Mía's side in the hospital, through every treatment, every procedure, every hard night, every moment of fear — and above all, through having to make incredibly difficult decisions. She's the one who holds her when she's in pain, who tries to make her smile when she has no strength left, and who makes sure that, even in the middle of all this, Mía can still feel like a little girl.

But her fight doesn't end when Mía falls asleep.

Many nights, when she should be resting, Sonia stays up researching.

She reads scientific studies. She searches for new treatments. She looks into hospitals, specialists, and cancer centers. She searches for clinical trials and breakthroughs happening not only in the United States, but anywhere in the world.

She asks, writes, calls, consults, compares, and researches again.

Because when it comes to her daughter's life, for Sonia there's no such thing as "I've already done enough," and no is never the final answer.

There's always one more question to ask, one more specialist to consult, one more study to read, or one more possibility still worth exploring.

That's Sonia. That's the mom behind Mía.

A mom who has put her own life on hold to become caregiver, researcher, companion, protector, and the most tireless voice for her little girl.

And she does all of this while her own health is also affected. Sonia lives with chronic fatigue and other health conditions that make each day even harder. And yet, despite the exhaustion, the pain, and her own limitations, she keeps pushing through to help, care for, defend, and protect Mía in her fight to save her life.

Many times she keeps going when her body is asking her to rest. She sets her own needs aside for later and finds strength where there seems to be none left, because she feels she can't give up while her daughter still needs her.

Sonia isn't only fighting Mía's cancer — she's also fighting her own exhaustion and health struggles. And still, every day, she gets back up and does everything she can to be there for her daughter.

And I am her mom.

As a mother, I wish I could lift every ounce of this weight off her shoulders. I can't fight this battle for her — but I can try to hold her up, so she can keep holding Mía up.

That's why I'm here, asking for help.

I don't want to ask from a place of pity. I want to ask from love, from hope, and from the certainty that no family should have to walk a road like this alone.

I want to be able to help my daughter so she can put all her strength into what matters most: caring for Mía, being there for every treatment, continuing to search for every possibility that exists, and giving her little girl the best chance to heal and to grow.

This illness has completely changed our lives and brought enormous emotional, physical, and financial responsibilities with it. Treatments, travel, appointments, research, medications, and everyday needs continue while Sonia devotes her time and strength to caring for Mía — even while her own health is affected.

That's why today we're opening our hearts and our story.

If you can help financially, thank you.

If you can share this page, thank you.

If you know doctors, researchers, institutions, foundations, or resources that could help us, thank you.

If you can offer support in any way, thank you.

Whatever you can do — even sending a prayer, a blessing, or a hopeful thought for Mía — we receive it with our whole heart.

Every person who shares, every person who helps, and every door that opens means Sonia doesn't have to carry this weight alone.

My wish as a grandmother is to watch Mía grow up.

And my wish as a mother is to be able to hold up my daughter while she fights, with a love that knows no limits, the most important battle of her life: her little girl's life.

We don't know where the next blessing will come from. Maybe from a doctor who reads our story. Maybe from someone who knows of a research breakthrough. Maybe from someone who can help us financially. Maybe simply from someone who shares this page and it reaches just the right person.

That's why we're asking:

Help us get Mía's story as far as it can possibly go.

Because as long as there's a possibility, Sonia will keep searching for it.

As long as there's a door, she'll try to open it.

And as long as Mía keeps fighting, we'll keep fighting right beside her.

With love, faith, and deep gratitude,

Sonia Celpa Mía's grandmother and mom of Sonia Rivas
